How I Think

Good systems begin before the software.

My process starts by understanding the actual work—where people repeat, hesitate, lose information, make avoidable mistakes, or spend too much time.

01

Find the friction.

Observe the parts of the workflow that repeatedly waste time, create confusion, or depend too much on memory.

02

Map the logic.

Translate actual project rules, inputs, approvals, relationships, and outputs into a clear workflow architecture.

03

Design for reality.

Build around real users, real constraints, real reports, and the way work is actually performed—not idealized theory.

04

Make it repeatable.

Turn one successful solution into a reliable system that can be reused, reviewed, improved, and scaled.

The Story

I understand the work because I have done the work.

Before building digital systems, I spent more than a decade coordinating, monitoring, inspecting, documenting, and reporting construction projects.

I worked across field execution, planning support, industrial construction, public infrastructure, accomplishment validation, time-related documents, variation orders, progress reporting, and multi-project coordination.

That experience now shapes the systems I create. I focus on clarity, traceability, practical controls, and outputs that engineers and project teams can actually use.
